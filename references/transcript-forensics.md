# Transcript attribution

Use for earnings calls, investor days, management interviews or expert calls when investigating information and price relevance. A private call cannot explain public-market reaction without evidence of when its information reached the market. Keep proprietary source text outside public artifacts.

Retain original text/language, speaker, prepared-remarks/Q&A section, page/line/time locator, reporting period, call/publication time and retrieval date. Transcription uncertainty remains visible. Translation and cleaned prose supplement, not replace, original evidence.

For each factor:

1. Quote the disclosure. Analyst-question premises are not verified company facts; distinguish a question from management's answer. An unanswered question is uncertainty, not confirmation.
2. Compare with the already-published release, prior guidance, company news and calls. Mark `new_information`, `already_disclosed` or `unknown`. Link the earliest public disclosure and any preliminary update; later repetition may confirm expectations but is not newly revealed without evidence.
3. Document a prior expectation and its pre-publication cutoff, comparable unit, period and accounting basis. Never infer numeric expectations from tone, current estimates or later price action.
4. State the mechanism: volume/price/mix → revenue → margin/cash, or risk/capital returns → valuation. The mechanism is an inference. Earnings/multiple decomposition needs comparable positive denominators and dated estimates; do not force it for loss-making companies.
5. Calculate raw/benchmark reactions using the event-window reference. Daily data measure the combined disclosure window, not individual remarks or isolated call effects.
6. Review alternative explanations and counterexamples, including same-window company releases, reputable news, macro/industry moves and broad/sector benchmark returns. Tone alone establishes neither intent nor price impact. Explain why a positive update might coexist with a negative price reaction. ETF price returns give market context; they do not establish fund flows.
7. Assign a grade and falsifier. `supported_driver` supports contribution, not a measured share of the move. Unknown timing or missing prior expectations excludes that grade.

Across quarters, track dominant questions, new concerns, substantive answer changes and metric definitions. Cite passages supporting tone descriptions, allow mixed/ambiguous interpretations and consider speaker changes. Include events with little price reaction; repeated metric surprises without relative reaction can refute a driver hypothesis.

The report leads with findings and uncertainty, then provides:

| Factor | Located evidence | New/known | Prior expectation | Mechanism | Raw/benchmark reaction | Alternatives | Grade | Falsifier |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

End with core disputes, both sides' evidence, next observable data and what changes the interpretation. A transcript-only request can deliver factor hypotheses and missing-price disclosures. Use [evidence-schema.md](evidence-schema.md) for JSON. Validation checks evidence links/eligibility, not source truth; semantic source review remains necessary.
